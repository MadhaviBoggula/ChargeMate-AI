Pricing Policy and Charger Technical Specifications

Tariff structure:
Price charging per kilowatt-hour (kWh) delivered. AC chargers usually carry a
lower per-kWh rate than DC fast chargers because they deliver less power and
cost less to operate. Keep one clear price per charger and display it
consistently in the app and on site.

Changing prices:
Price changes apply to future sessions only; never retroactively change the
price recorded on a completed session. Announce planned price changes to
drivers before they take effect. When running a promotional rate, record the
start and end time so revenue reports remain explainable.

Revenue reporting:
Revenue equals delivered energy multiplied by the session price per kWh.
Reports in this system are derived from completed charging sessions, not from
bookings. A cancelled or expired booking contributes no revenue.

Charger specifications:
AC chargers typically deliver 7 to 22 kW and suit overnight or workplace
charging. DC fast chargers typically deliver 50 to 150 kW and suit short
stops on long journeys. Connector power ratings must not exceed the charger's
rated output. CCS2 is the common DC connector; Type 2 is the common AC
connector; CHAdeMO appears on some older vehicles.

Capacity planning:
Estimate site capacity from the number of chargers, their rated power, and
the available electrical supply. Never add a charger that exceeds the site's
approved supply. If several DC chargers share a supply, use load management
so simultaneous sessions do not trip the main breaker.
